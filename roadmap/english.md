## EN 1 — Grammar (your weak points first)
**L1 — aware & can use**
- ⬜ **1.1 Articles** — a / an / the, zero article, countable vs uncountable nouns, first mention vs known thing, "a roadmap" vs "the roadmap", "the database", uncountables (data, information, feedback, advice, software)
- ⬜ **1.2 Prepositions** — depend on, participate in, work on (a project), write code in (Go), responsible for, interested in, familiar with, experience with/in, at/in/on for time and place
- ⬜ **1.3 Tenses for work talk** — past simple (finished projects: "I built X"), present perfect (experience: "I've worked with…"), present continuous (current work), present simple (habits, facts), future (will / going to), past continuous (background)
- ⬜ **1.4 Subject–verb agreement** — plural subject → plural verb ("some skills don't matter"), "the data is/are", "each of them is", "a number of … are", "the team is", "there is / there are"
- ⬜ **1.5 Modals** — should, could, might, must, would, can, may — for suggestions, uncertainty, obligation, polite requests ("could you take a look…")

**L2 — deep & efficient**
- ⬜ **1.6 Conditionals** — zero, first, second ("if I had more time, I would…"), third ("if we had added an index, it wouldn't have…"), mixed, unless, as long as, provided that — needed for trade-off talk
- ⬜ **1.7 Passive voice** — "the request is handled by…", "the data was migrated", "it has been deployed", when passive sounds natural (systems) vs active (your own actions in interviews)
- ⬜ **1.8 Relative clauses & linking words** — which / that / who / whose, defining vs non-defining clauses, however, therefore, whereas, although, as a result, on the other hand, in addition, instead

🛠 **Practice** — L1: write 5 sentences about your current project that use a/the correctly and three different tenses; check them against `notes/english/vocabulary.md` · L2: describe one design decision using second and third conditionals ("If we had chosen…, we would have…") in 5 sentences, out loud then written

## EN 4 — Interview phrases
**L1 — aware & can use**
- ⬜ **4.1 Thinking, clarifying, not knowing, trade-offs, disagreeing** — "let me think through this", "before I answer, can I ask…", "I haven't used that directly, but…", "the trade-off here is…", "my instinct would be X because…" — see the phrases table in [Method](#method)
- ⬜ **4.2 Behavioral (STAR)** — situation, task, action, result; "I took ownership of…", "the outcome was…", "what I learned was…", "looking back, I would…"
- ⬜ **4.3 Questions to the interviewer** — "What does a typical week look like…", "How do you handle on-call?", "What would success look like in the first 6 months?", "How are technical decisions made?"

**L2 — deep & efficient**
- ⬜ **4.4 Narrating system design** — "let's start with requirements", "at this scale…", "the bottleneck would be…", "to handle this, I'd introduce…", "let me zoom in on…", "if we needed to scale further…"
- ⬜ **4.5 Recovering under pressure** — "actually, let me revise that", "I'm thinking out loud here", "could you repeat the question?", "just to make sure I understood…", buying time naturally

🛠 **Practice** — L1: answer "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate" out loud in STAR form, under 2 minutes, using 3 phrases from the table · L2: narrate a URL-shortener design out loud for 10 minutes, recording it; count how many signposting phrases you used

## EN 5 — Writing
**L1 — aware & can use**
- ⬜ **5.1 Commit messages & PR descriptions** — imperative mood ("add", not "added"), short summary line, body explains why, "Fixes #123", PR template (what / why / how tested)
- ⬜ **5.2 Code review comments** — "nit:", "consider…", "what do you think about…", "LGTM", "non-blocking:", "could we…?", asking instead of commanding, praising good code
- ⬜ **5.3 Slack / chat messages** — concise asks, "heads-up", "FYI", "blocked on…", "no rush", context-first messages, threads, status updates

**L2 — deep & efficient**
- ⬜ **5.4 Design docs & technical emails** — context, goals, non-goals, proposal, alternatives considered, risks, open questions, formal email structure, polite follow-ups
- ⬜ **5.5 Incident reports & postmortems** — timeline, impact, root cause, contributing factors, action items, blameless language ("the deploy caused…", not "X broke…")

🛠 **Practice** — L1: rewrite your last 5 commit messages in imperative mood with a why-line; write 3 review comments for a teammate's PR using softening phrases · L2: write a 1-page postmortem (in English) for a real or invented incident from your work

## Method

**Daily minimum:** learn **one** term / phrase / grammar point and use it in your own sentence. Log it in `LOG.md`.
**What to pick:** this roadmap → next ⬜ subtopic.
**Corrections & vocabulary:** `notes/english/vocabulary.md` (only correct versions).

### Daily (10–15 min)
- Read one technical blog post (Go Blog, Cloudflare, Stripe Engineering)
- Note 2–3 new technical phrases in `notes/english/vocabulary.md`
- Once per week: say your project description out loud in English

### Shadowing (3x/week, 10 min)
- Source: Go podcasts (Fallthrough, Cup o' Go), conference talks (GopherCon)
- Method: listen 30 sec → pause → repeat exactly, same rhythm and intonation
- Target: less hesitation when speaking under interview pressure

### Interview English — must-know phrases

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Thinking | "Let me think through this..." / "Give me a moment to reason about this..." |
| Clarifying | "Before I answer, can I ask — what's the scale we're designing for?" |
| Don't know | "I haven't used that directly, but here's how I'd approach it..." |
| Trade-off | "The trade-off here is..." / "This approach gives us X, but costs us Y..." |
| Disagreeing | "That's a valid approach. My instinct would be X because..." |
| Stuck | "I'm thinking out loud here — one approach would be..." |
| Mistake | "Actually, let me revise that — I think the better answer is..." |

### Transfer errors to eliminate (Uzbek → English)
- **Articles:** "the database", "a complete roadmap" — always check a/an/the before nouns
- **Prepositions:** "depends **on**", "work **on** a project", "participate **in**", "write code **in** Go"
- **Tense:** past for finished work ("I built X") vs present perfect for experience ("I've worked with...")
- **Agreement:** "some skills **don't** matter" (plural subject → plural verb)
- **Word order:** adjective before noun ("slow query", not "query slow")
